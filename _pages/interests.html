The general direction is computational mathematics. This is because I really enjoyed the computational methods course this semester and I myself am also somewhat interested in algorithms. In the future, I would like to focus more on this area. In computational mathematics, I am a bit hesitant about the specific subfields. Currently, I am considering numerical optimization and image processing. Considering the rise of AI, I hope my future research direction will be related to AI, machine learning, and neural networks. Additionally, I am very willing to study operations research and control theory.

PhD Intention

I'm not sure whether I will work or delve deeply into academic research. The latter requires a doctoral degree, and currently, I lean towards a master's degree, more oriented towards employment. However, I'm not opposed to the academic path either. This might be more based on my experience as a graduate student. I want to test whether I am suitable for the academic path before deciding whether to pursue a doctoral degree. I also hope to obtain a doctoral degree if this path suits me. The recent news that Wang Hong and Deng Yu won the Fields Medal is so inspiring. I can't help imagining myself becoming a pure mathematician and making some wonderful mathematical achievements. At this stage, I am more confused about the future. But I believe that with effort, the outcome won't be too bad.

Future Plans

I have two main paths for the future. One is to enter the industry directly after earning a master's degree — working at a major tech company always sounds very appealing. If I find genuine enjoyment during my master's studies, I would also be very willing to pursue a PhD. As for the PhD, I hope to pursue it abroad. I want to see the world during the most precious years of my life, broaden my horizons, and engage with more cutting-edge mathematics.
